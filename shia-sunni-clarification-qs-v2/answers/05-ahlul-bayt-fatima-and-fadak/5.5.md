## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

The book in question is *Kitab Sulaym ibn Qays*, widely described as the
oldest surviving Shia hadith compilation, attributed to Sulaym ibn Qays
al-Hilali, said to be an associate of Ali [R1]. Its content includes
material on Saqifah, the Fadak dispute, the companions' opposition to
Ali, and the claim that Umar's actions against Fatima's household
contributed to her death [R2]. The question's framing raises a real and
specific rijal issue, worth taking apart carefully rather than accepting
or dismissing wholesale, per Principle 1.

**The transmission chain, as the traditional account has it:** Sulaym
fled to Persia around 694 CE to escape al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf, the Umayyad
governor notorious for persecuting associates of Ali; in this account he
sheltered with a young man, Aban ibn Abi Ayyash, taught him this
material, and Aban became the book's principal transmitter [R3]. This
detail in the question ("in whose house the former hid from Hajjaj bin
Yusuf") matches the traditional narrative found in search results this
session, with one caveat worth being precise about: the more commonly
reported version has Sulaym fleeing to Aban's protection, i.e. Aban
sheltering Sulaym, not the reverse; the question's exact phrasing is
ambiguous on who hid in whose house, and this dossier reads it as
consistent with the standard account rather than contradicting it [R3].

**On Aban ibn Abi Ayyash's reliability, the harshest documented
condemnation found this session is from Shu'ba ibn al-Hajjaj, not
explicitly from Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (the compiler of Sahih Muslim).**
Shu'ba is quoted saying "if Ibn Abi Ayyash did not narrate false
traditions I would give my garments away in charity," and, more sharply,
that he would rather drink from his donkey's urine than say "Aban told
me" [R4]. That is a severe rijal verdict, and it is real. What this
dossier could not independently confirm is the specific claim that
*Sahih Muslim* (the book, or Imam Muslim as its compiler) is the source
of a "liar" verdict on Aban. It is entirely possible Aban is simply
absent from Sahih Muslim's chains, meaning Muslim did not rely on him,
which is a different claim from Muslim explicitly branding him a liar in
a surviving rijal statement; both would fairly be summarized in casual
speech as "Sahih Muslim doesn't accept him," but only the second is what
the question specifically asserts, and this dossier could not verify it
at that precision this session.

**Per Principle 1, the negative grading itself deserves scrutiny, not
just acceptance.** Aban's known biography gives a concrete reason to ask
whether his weak grading is purely a hadith-methodology judgment or
partly colored by his open Shia sympathies and his documented role
sheltering a man al-Hajjaj's Umayyad administration wanted arrested [R3].
This is not a claim that the grading is therefore wrong, rijal critics of
the period graded plenty of narrators on genuinely observed
inconsistency or weak memory, unconnected to politics, but a narrator
whose defining biographical fact is hiding a persecuted Alid associate
from an Umayyad governor is exactly the kind of case Principle 5's
money/power lens says is worth flagging for a closer look, not waving
past. No such closer look has been done here; it is logged as an open
question, not a conclusion.

**Notably, even Shia rijal scholarship is divided on Aban and the
book, which cuts against reading this purely as a Sunni-versus-Shia
dispute.** Classical Shia scholars including al-Najashi and al-Tusi
accepted the book as genuinely ancient; other scholars, including some
contemporary ones, reject it as fabricated, citing among other reasons
Aban's own weakness as a narrator [R5]. Some Shia specialists also note
that the book's transmission is not solely dependent on Aban, other
chains exist alongside his, which partially, not fully, mitigates the
concern about relying on a single weak transmitter [R5].

### The Shia position, after this research

The strongest form of this argument does not deny Aban's weak grading, it
argues the book does not rest on him alone [R5], and that his negative
rijal verdict, coming from a period and a scholarly circle operating
under or adjacent to the same Umayyad power structure his patron Sulaym
was fleeing, deserves the same "who is grading, and why" scrutiny this
project applies to every other narrator-criticism, per Principle 1 and
Principle 5, rather than being treated as a neutral, apolitical
verdict that settles the book's content.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

The mainstream Sunni response does not need to overreach into an
unconfirmed "Sahih Muslim calls him a liar" claim to make its point: Aban
ibn Abi Ayyash's weakness is independently and severely attested by
Shu'ba, one of the most rigorous early hadith critics [R4], and the
book's core, most serious allegation, that Umar's actions caused Fatima's
death, rests overwhelmingly on this one chain of transmission in its
earliest layer, with even some Shia scholars themselves rejecting the
book partly for this reason [R5]. On this view, a report this
consequential, alleging a companion caused the death of the Prophet's
daughter, deserves more than one weak chain before being treated as
established, regardless of which tradition is doing the weighing.

### What would resolve this

Direct access to a rijal database (al-Dhahabi's *Mizan al-I'tidal*, Ibn
Hajar's *Tahdhib al-Tahdhib* or *Taqrib al-Tahdhib*, or Sahih Muslim's own
*Muqaddimah*/*Kitab al-Tamyiz*) to confirm or correct the specific "Sahih
Muslim calls him a liar" attribution, rather than this dossier's
substitution of the Shu'ba condemnation it could confirm; direct access
to the alternate chains for *Kitab Sulaym ibn Qays* that Ayatullah Milani
and others reference, to assess how independent they really are of Aban;
and a documented check of what period and scholarly circle produced the
earliest negative verdicts on Aban, to apply Principle 5 properly rather
than flag it as an open question.

material as unconfirmed rather than silently substituting one citation
for another.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 1** is applied directly and is the center of this dossier:
Aban's negative rijal grading is treated as a claim to examine (who made
it, on what basis, and does the specific attribution to Sahih Muslim
actually hold up) rather than accepted at face value because it appears
in the source question. **Principle 2** applies by extension: the same
scrutiny given to Bukhari and Abu Hurairah elsewhere in this project is
given here to the classical rijal critics themselves, their verdicts are
not treated as self-authenticating just because they are old or
widely repeated. **Principle 5** is applied as an explicitly flagged open
question rather than a conclusion: Aban's documented role sheltering a
persecuted Alid associate from Umayyad authority is noted as a plausible
motive-relevant fact worth investigating regarding who graded him
negatively and under what circumstances, without asserting that
connection as proven.
