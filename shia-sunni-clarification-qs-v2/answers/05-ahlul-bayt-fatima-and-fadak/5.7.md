## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

**A citation correction, stated plainly first, per the Researcher
Protocol's rule on correcting the compilation's own errors without
editing the verbatim source:** the chapter file, at both §4.5/§4.8 and
here at §5.7, links "Sahih al-Bukhari 6787" and describes it as the
inheritance-restriction hadith ("we prophets do not leave inheritance").
That is not what Bukhari 6787 says. Bukhari 6787, in the Book of Hudood
(Limits and Punishments), is narrated by Aisha and concerns the
Makhzumiyyah woman who committed theft: the Prophet refused Usama's
intercession on her behalf and declared that nations before the Muslims
were destroyed because they punished the weak and excused the powerful,
continuing (in the closely linked following hadith, Bukhari 6788) with
"By Allah, if Fatima, the daughter of Muhammad, had stolen, I would have
cut off her hand" [R1]. The actual "we prophets do not leave inheritance"
hadith, the one this project already relies on at [5.2](5.2.md), is
Bukhari 6730, in the Book of Fara'id (Laws of Inheritance) [R2]. This
project has not edited the chapter's verbatim text, per protocol, but
this dossier records the correction here and treats the argument below
using the hadith the source material actually meant, the inheritance
hadith [R2], while also, separately, taking the hadith it actually cited,
6787/6788 [R1], on its own terms, because it turns out to be relevant to
this exact question in its own right, addressed below.

**The argument as posed is explicitly conditional, and that framing
should be preserved, not resolved into a verdict this dossier does not
have the standing to give.** The question states its premises up front:
"assuming this hadith is true... and Fadak was in fact stolen." Whether
those premises hold is exactly what [5.2](5.2.md) and [5.3](5.3.md)
investigate; this dossier does not re-litigate that, it tests whether the
conditional's conclusion follows *if* the premises are granted, which is
a question of internal logical consistency, not of historical fact.

**Given the premises, does the "don't judge the companions" defense
survive?** The companions-justice framework, examined in detail at
[Chapter 4, §4.8](../../chapters/04-sahaba-companions-and-disputes.md)
and its answer dossier, generally rests on Qur'an 9:100's praise of the
early Muslims and a broader principle that the first generation should
not be second-guessed by later observers [R3]. But that framework was
never presented, in either tradition's own strongest form, as an
exemption from justice for demonstrated wrongdoing, it is a presumption
of good faith and high standing, which is a different thing from
immunity. The hadith the source material actually cited, even though
misidentified by number, makes this distinction explicit and does so by
naming Fatima specifically: the Prophet's stated principle in Bukhari
6787/6788 is that legal accountability must apply without exception for
personal closeness or status, "even if Fatima, the daughter of Muhammad"
stole, the punishment would still apply [R1]. Taken on its own terms,
that is a hadith about equal accountability regardless of who is
involved, and it sits logically upstream of the question being asked: if
the Prophet's own stated principle rejects giving Fatima a pass because
of who she is, a "don't judge the companions" defense that gives her
alleged wrongers a pass because of who *they* are would need independent
justification beyond "they were companions," not less.

**The second citation, Sahih Muslim 1978a, adds a more specific and
directly on-point principle for a land dispute.** This hadith, via Amir
ibn Wathila from Ali, records the Prophet stating several things told to
him "in secret" that carry a curse: cursed is the one who sacrifices for
other than Allah, cursed is the one who curses his parents, cursed is the
one who shelters an innovator, and cursed is the one who alters land
boundary markers [R4]. That last clause is the one doing the work in
this argument: a hadith explicitly cursing the fraudulent alteration of
land boundaries is directly on point for a dispute about the wrongful
seizure of a specific piece of land, Fadak, in a way the source
material's chain of citations (mislabeled hadith number aside) clearly
intends, and this dossier had not previously connected that specific
clause to the Fadak dispute before this research pass; it is a genuinely
apt citation for the argument being made, more precisely apt than the
mislabeled 6787 citation turns out to be.

**Ash-Shura 42:40 supplies the argument's closing move, and it cuts in
a more measured direction than a first read suggests.** The verse states
that recompense for a wrong should be proportionate to the wrong, but
that forgiving and making reconciliation is better, with that reward
belonging to Allah, and that Allah does not love wrongdoers [R5]. Used as
the question's closing citation, this does not straightforwardly demand
punishment of "everyone involved in asserting/justifying the actions of
the oppressors," as the question's own wording puts it, it actually
argues the opposite emphasis: proportionate accountability is
permitted, but forgiveness is explicitly framed as the better and
Allah-rewarded path. If anything, citing 42:40 to close this argument
cuts against a demand for retroactive judgment on everyone who
historically justified the Fadak decision, and toward the same
forgiveness-oriented posture Sunni scholarship generally takes when
discussing this period, an outcome the question's own concluding
citation does not obviously support the framing it is attached to.

### The Shia position, after this research

The strongest form of this argument does not need the mislabeled Bukhari
citation to work, it needs three things that do hold up under this
research: that the inheritance-refusal itself is securely attested in
Bukhari [R2] (established already at 5.2), that the Prophet's own
recorded standard for accountability rejects exempting even Fatima
personally from consequence for wrongdoing [R1], and that a specific,
independently real hadith curses exactly the kind of land-boundary
injustice a wrongful Fadak seizure would represent [R4]. From those three
premises, if the further premise that Fadak was wrongfully withheld is
granted, a "don't judge the companions" defense that operates as a
blanket exemption rather than a rebuttable presumption does not
straightforwardly follow from anything in the Qur'an or Sunni hadith
corpus itself, on this reading; it would need to be argued for
independently, not simply invoked.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

The fair Sunni response starts by noting the citation error: an argument
built partly on "Bukhari 6787, the inheritance hadith" is built on a
misidentified source, and a careful reader should notice a hadith about
a different topic (equal punishment for theft) being repurposed for an
inheritance argument, even though, as this dossier finds, it turns out to
be thematically relevant on its own separate merits. Beyond that, the
"don't judge the companions" position, at its strongest, does not claim
companions are exempt from the moral content of accountability
principles like the ones in 6787/6788 or Muslim 1978a, it claims that a
later observer, without full access to the companions' intentions,
circumstances, and the legal reasoning available to them at the time, is
poorly positioned to convict them retroactively, particularly regarding a
ruling (the inheritance hadith) that Abu Bakr sincerely held to be
binding legal instruction rather than a personal decision against Fatima,
as already discussed at [5.2](5.2.md) [R2]. And Ash-Shura 42:40's actual
emphasis, forgiveness as the preferred and rewarded path over exacting
proportionate recompense [R5], is read by this position as supporting
restraint in retroactive judgment, not demanding it, which the question's
own closing citation does not clearly support in the direction it is
deployed.

### What would resolve this

Primary confirmation of Bukhari 6730's exact wording and chain for the
inheritance hadith, since this dossier's correction depends on that
identification being right; primary confirmation of Sahih Muslim 1978a's
exact wording (this dossier relies on search-result triangulation, not a
direct read) to confirm the land-boundary-marker clause is worded as
described; and, most importantly, a resolved answer from [5.2](5.2.md)
and [5.3](5.3.md) on whether Fadak actually was wrongfully withheld,
since this entire dossier explicitly operates on that premise as
conditional rather than established, and nothing here should be read as
resolving that underlying question.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 1** is applied to the citation itself, not just to a
narrator's rijal grading: the source material's identification of
Bukhari 6787 as the inheritance hadith was checked rather than passed

linked citation in this project's own source material needs the same
"don't take it at face value" scrutiny applied to classical sources.
**Principle 4** is applied to the question's own logical argument: it is
treated as a structured conditional claim to test for internal
consistency given its stated premises, not adopted or rejected as a
whole because of who raised it or how it is worded. The Researcher

unedited, and the correction is recorded here in the research layer, as
