## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

The chapter file presents this as one continuous sermon transcribed from
two sibtayn.com images. Research this session finds the two portions do
not actually carry equal attestation, and that distinction matters for
answering "is it authentic" honestly rather than as a single yes/no.

**The chapter's "closing portion"** ("O' Prophet of Allah! Please accept
my salutation and those of your daughter who is being buried not far
from you...") matches Sermon 202 of Nahj al-Balagha, titled in most
English editions "What the Commander of the Faithful said on the
occasion of the burial of Fatima, while addressing the Prophet at his
grave" [R1]. This is a securely placed text: it appears in essentially
every recension of Nahj al-Balagha (the sermon number shifts slightly
between editions, but the text itself is stable) [R1], and, more
significantly for authenticity, it is independently attested outside
Nahj al-Balagha and outside exclusively Shia sources. It is reported in
Shia hadith works including al-Kulayni's *al-Kafi* and al-Tusi's
*al-Amali*, and in a Sunni-authored work, Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's *Tadhkirat
al-Khawass* [R2]. A text carried by an independent Sunni historian as
well as Shia hadith compilations, predating or independent of Sharif
al-Radi's compilation of Nahj al-Balagha itself, is meaningfully better
attested than a text found only inside Nahj al-Balagha with no earlier or
outside corroboration. Recent academic work on Nahj al-Balagha's
authenticity generally, tracking its sermons in sources predating al-
Radi's compilation, supports this kind of case-by-case rather than
blanket verdict: some material traces earlier, some does not [R3].

**The chapter's "opening portion"** ("O' Apostle of God! Your dear
daughter will tell you how your followers have behaved with her...") is a
different matter. This dossier could not locate this specific text as
part of Nahj al-Balagha's Sermon 202 or any other numbered sermon in the
standard collection, nor trace it to al-Kafi, al-Amali, or Tadhkirat
al-Khawass in the way the closing portion is traced. It reads as
devotional literature in circulation on Shia commemorative sites
(sibtayn.com among them, per the chapter's own sourcing) without a
classical citation this dossier could confirm. That does not make it
fabricated outright, a great deal of authentic devotional material
circulates without every phrase being individually chain-traced, but it
means the honest confidence level for this portion is markedly lower
than for the portion matching Sermon 202, and the chapter's presentation
of both as "Quoted from Nahj al-Balagha" (per the authenticity note that
followed the images) appears to overstate the sourcing of at least the
opening portion specifically.

**On authenticity more broadly:** the underlying historical claim, that
Ali privately grieved at Fatima's grave and addressed the Prophet there,
is plausible and unremarkable as an event; the live question is textual,
whether the specific words transmitted are Ali's own, and that question
has a different answer for each of the two portions the chapter combines
into one sermon.

### The Shia position, after this research

The strongest form of this text's authenticity claim rests on the
closing portion, which really is well attested: it is stable across Nahj
al-Balagha's manuscript tradition, corroborated in earlier Shia hadith
works, and, notably, also carried by a Sunni historian working
independently of the Shia devotional tradition [R1] [R2]. That
cross-tradition attestation is a genuinely strong point, not a weak one
dressed up, and it should be argued on that specific strength rather than
folded together with the less-attested opening portion.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

Mainstream Sunni scholarship's general posture toward Nahj al-Balagha is
not blanket rejection, some material, including sermons like this one, is
independently attested in sources Sunni scholars themselves compiled
(Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's *Tadhkirat al-Khawass* among them) [R2], while other
material in the collection, particularly more theologically loaded or
eschatological passages, has drawn specific Sunni scholarly rejection
historically (Khatib al-Baghdadi's objections being a known example) [R3].
The fair Sunni response to this specific sermon is not to deny Ali
grieved at his wife's grave, but to note that this project's own research
found one of the two "halves" the chapter presents as a single sermon
lacks the same corroboration as the other, and that distinction should be
preserved rather than treating both halves as equally certain because
they were transcribed from the same webpage.

### What would resolve this

Direct access to a primary critical edition of Nahj al-Balagha to confirm
Sermon 202's exact wording and confirm it matches the chapter's "closing
portion" precisely, not just in general theme; direct access to
al-Kulayni's *al-Kafi*, al-Tusi's *al-Amali*, and Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's
*Tadhkirat al-Khawass* to confirm each independently carries this sermon
as claimed; and a specific search for the chapter's "opening portion"
text in Shia du'a/ziyarat literature (Mafatih al-Jinan and similar later
compilations are a plausible source and have not been checked) to
establish whether it has any classical sourcing at all or is a later
devotional composition.

## References

- **R1**: Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon 202 (numbering varies slightly by
  edition), on Imam Ali's words at Fatima's burial addressing the
  Prophet. **Medium-high confidence** that this sermon is a real, stably
  transmitted part of the Nahj al-Balagha manuscript tradition and
  matches the chapter's "closing portion" in theme and substantial
  content; the exact wording has not been independently checked against
  a primary critical edition this session.
- **R2**: Corroboration of Sermon 202 in al-Kulayni's *al-Kafi*, al-Tusi's
  *al-Amali*, and the Sunni historian Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's *Tadhkirat
  al-Khawass*. **Medium confidence** that this specific cross-tradition
  corroboration claim is accurate (it is a recurring, specific claim
  across multiple search results, not a vague "it's well known"); none of
  the three works has been opened directly this session to confirm.
- **R3**: General academic and classical scholarly assessment of Nahj
  al-Balagha's authenticity as mixed and case-by-case rather than
  uniform, including Khatib al-Baghdadi's specific rejection of some
  (not all) sermons. **Medium confidence** on the general characterization;
  not traced to a specific academic source or page this session.

against a real text, not as independently confirmed.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 1** applies directly: rather than accepting the chapter's
single "Quoted from Nahj al-Balagha" attribution at face value for both
portions, this dossier tested that claim and found it holds solidly for
one portion and not, on available evidence, for the other, splitting a
single authenticity verdict into two rather than passing the weaker half
through on the strength of the stronger one. **Principle 4** applies to
the sibtayn.com framing note itself ("To check authenticity"), which
this dossier treats as an open instruction to verify, consistent with
how the project treats all forwarded material as context rather than
settled fact.
