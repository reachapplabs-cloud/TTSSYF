# 2.2 — Where are Imam Ja'far's ahadith, and why aren't they taught?

## Answer

The question splits into two distinct sub-questions with different
answers, which the summary below keeps separate rather than blending:

1. **Ja'far al-Sadiq is not broadly excluded as an unreliable narrator.**
   He is cited in *Muwatta Malik*, and Malik ibn Anas is on record with a
   direct positive rijal verdict: "al-thiqah Ja'far ibn Muhammad" ("the
   trustworthy Ja'far ibn Muhammad"). He is widely reported (though not
   independently number-confirmed by this project) to be narrated from
   in the Sunan collections too. The one specific, real exclusion is
   **Sahih al-Bukhari** — but Bukhari cited him in his separate work
   *Al-Adab al-Mufrad*, which undercuts a simple "considered untrustworthy"
   reading. Two competing explanations for the Sahih al-Bukhari exclusion
   specifically (a stated Sunni methodological preference for shorter
   chains, applied to al-Shafi'i too per al-Khatib al-Baghdadi; versus a
   Shia-sourced claim of political/theological caution) are both
   presented in the chapter, unresolved.
2. **The "Abu Hanifa and Malik studied under him" claim is real but more
   contested in its precise framing than either side's popular version.**
   Malik's praise-quote is solid; the claim that Abu Hanifa was formally
   his student is pushed back on by at least one Sunni source (IlmGate),
   which argues Abu Hanifa's real lineage runs through Hammad ibn Abi
   Sulayman → Ibrahim al-Nakha'i → Ibn Mas'ud.
3. **The likely real answer to "why isn't he taught from"** is conceptual:
   Sunni Islam cites him as a link transmitting the Prophet's ﷺ words
   (which happens), but doesn't treat his own independent rulings as an
   authoritative source of law the way Twelver Shia jurisprudence does —
   because that second kind of authority rests on Imamate doctrine, which
   Sunni Islam doesn't accept. That is also why the majority of Twelver
   hadith collections trace to him specifically: he is that tradition's
   central living legal authority, not merely a transmitter within it.
   Noted as a check against reading this as targeted: several other
   contemporary jurist-traditions (al-Awza'i, al-Thawri, al-Zahiri) also
   didn't survive as living Sunni madhabs alongside the four that did.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 1** applies directly to the rijal question at the heart of
this item: Malik's "thiqah" verdict and Bukhari's Sahih al-Bukhari
exclusion are both presented as claims with their own reasoning, not
deferred to as automatically settled — especially since they appear to
point in different directions (one Sunni authority calling him reliable,
another Sunni collection omitting him), which is exactly the kind of
tension Principle 1 exists to surface rather than resolve by picking the
answer that's more convenient. **Principle 4** governs how the
Abu Hanifa/Malik "studied under him" claim is handled: presented with
its contested framing intact (a real relationship, disputed in how
formal it was) rather than adopted uncritically by either the
Shia-apologetic or Sunni-skeptical version.
