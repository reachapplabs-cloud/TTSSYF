## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

Sahih al-Bukhari contains more than Prophetic hadith (*marfu'*). It also
contains *mawquf* narrations (a Companion's own words or actions, not
attributed to the Prophet), *maqtu'* narrations (attributed only to a
Tabi'i, a Successor), and *mu'allaq* narrations (chains missing one or
more narrators at the start), 1,341 of these in total, though most are
backed by a full chain elsewhere in the same book; Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani
catalogued the 160 that are not backed elsewhere anywhere in the Sahih,
in his dedicated work Taghliq al-Ta'liq, specifically to locate or
assess outside chains for them, and he is reported to have argued he
succeeded in tracing full, connected chains for all 160 [R1]. *Athar* is
the broader term often used for the mawquf/maqtu' material collectively.
Bukhari's chapter headings (*tarajim*) are also a distinctive structural
feature, they frequently embed his own juristic reasoning, Qur'an verses,
and Companion statements, meaning a meaningful part of his scholarly
position is expressed through how chapters are titled and organized, not
only through the hadith text under them [R2]. This category question
matters before any authenticity debate even starts, treating a mawquf
Companion opinion as if it carried Prophetic weight, or vice versa,
misreads what a given citation is actually claiming.

On the numbers: Bukhari is widely reported to have examined roughly
600,000 narrations over about sixteen years, the questioner's "700,000
odd" figure sits in the same range commonly cited across sources, which
vary [R3]. What made it into the book is usually put at ~7,563 hadith
counting repetition, the same report counted again each time it supports
a different chapter, recounted variously as ~7,275 or, in a modern
recount by Khaldun al-Ahdab, 7,208, and ~2,602 unique narrations without
repetition, though some counts give closer to ~4,000 depending on how
near-identical wording variants are merged [R4]. Bukhari's own stated
criterion, widely quoted: "I only included in my book those that were
authentic, and I left out many more authentic traditions than this to
avoid unnecessary length" [R5].

That quote matters for how the ~600,000-to-~7,500 gap should actually be
read, and this is the point where this dossier makes its own explicit
interpretive judgment rather than only reporting figures: the gap is not
evidence that ~590,000-plus reports were judged fabricated. It includes
reports Bukhari considered weak or false, but also, by his own stated
words, a substantial number he considered authentic and simply excluded
for redundancy, brevity, or scope. Reading the gap as "so almost
everything was rejected as fake" overstates the case in one direction;
reading everything that made the final cut as therefore flawless because
it survived such brutal filtering overstates it in the other. Neither
follows from the figures alone, and this project should not adopt either
framing when citing Bukhari elsewhere.

### What this evidence supports, for this project's broader argument

This dossier supports treating "recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari" as the
start of a category-and-scrutiny question, not the end of one. Before
weighing whether a specific Bukhari citation is reliable, this project
should first check what kind of report it is, marfu', mawquf, maqtu', or
mu'allaq [R1] [R2], since only marfu' reports even claim to be the
Prophet's own words in the first place, and a citation's persuasive
weight depends on that category before it depends on anything else. It
also supports resisting both a purely apologetic and a purely polemical
reading of the ~600,000-to-~7,500 figure: the scale of the filtering is
real and should be taken seriously as evidence of a deliberate, stated
selection criterion [R3] [R4] [R5], but that criterion, authenticity plus
brevity, does not by itself tell this project how many of the ~590,000
excluded reports Bukhari actually judged inauthentic versus merely
redundant, and that gap in the record should stay explicit rather than
resolved by assumption in either direction.

### The strongest defense of Bukhari's selection method, on its own terms

Bukhari's own stated criterion is a real methodological commitment, not
a post-hoc justification: authenticity first, then active pruning for
brevity and redundancy, and he is on record naming that second step
explicitly rather than letting readers assume every excluded report was
excluded for the same reason [R5]. The mu'allaq material is a further
point in the method's favor rather than against it: rather than simply
omitting chain-truncated reports Bukhari judged still worth including for
other reasons, he flagged them structurally as truncated, and a later
scholar working entirely within the same tradition, Ibn Hajar, treated
that flag as an open research question worth a dedicated, systematic
work (Taghliq al-Ta'liq) rather than an embarrassment to paper over [R1].
A collection whose own internal structure marks its weaker links and
invites exactly the kind of follow-up scrutiny Ibn Hajar undertook is
doing something more transparent than a collection that simply presents
every report identically regardless of chain completeness.

### What would resolve this

Direct access to sunnah.com's collection-statistics page and Ibn Hajar's
Taghliq al-Ta'liq itself, or a secondary source that cites it with a page
number, for the exact mu'allaq count and Ibn Hajar's stated success rate
in tracing outside chains, rather than the search-result paraphrase used
here; a primary source (Ibn al-Salah's Muqaddimah, or a direct count from
sunnah.com) to settle the 7,563/7,275/7,208 with-repetition figure and
the 2,602-versus-~4,000 without-repetition figure to one confirmed
number rather than a reported range; and direct access to Bukhari's own
introduction or a primary biographical source (al-Khatib al-Baghdadi's
Tarikh Baghdad, or al-Dhahabi's Siyar A'lam al-Nubala) for the exact
wording and context of his "I only included those that were authentic"
statement, rather than the widely-quoted but not independently traced
version used here.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 4** applies to this dossier's own conclusion about the
"~600,000 to ~7,500" gap: the popular framing on both the apologetic side
("brutal 99% filtering proves rigor") and the critical side ("so almost
everything was rejected as fake") is flagged as an oversimplification not
supported by Bukhari's own stated criterion, rather than adopting either
framing uncritically. **Principle 1** is the broader point this answer
exists to serve: knowing that Bukhari contains mawquf/maqtu'/mu'allaq
material alongside marfu' hadith is a precondition for correctly weighing
any specific "Sahih al-Bukhari" citation elsewhere in this project, a
citation's category, is it even claiming to be the Prophet's own words,
should be checked before its authenticity is debated. **Principle 3**
applies to the mu'allaq material specifically: a chain missing its
opening narrators is, structurally, a form of truncation, and this
dossier treats Ibn Hajar's dedicated follow-up work on the 160
unconfirmed cases as the tradition's own acknowledgment that truncated
chains are worth chasing down rather than passing over.
