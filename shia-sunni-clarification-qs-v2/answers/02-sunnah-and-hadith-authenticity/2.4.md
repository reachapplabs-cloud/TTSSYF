## Answer

See [Chapter 2, §2.4](../../chapters/02-sunnah-and-hadith-authenticity.md)
for the full answer. Summary: Sahih al-Bukhari contains marfu' (Prophetic),
mawquf (Companion-only), maqtu' (Successor-only), and mu'allaq (chain-
truncated — 1,341 total, 160 without a full chain elsewhere in the book,
per Ibn Hajar's *Taghliq al-Ta'liq*) material, plus juristically-loaded
chapter headings. Bukhari is reported to have examined ~600,000
narrations and included ~7,563 with repetition (~2,602 unique) — a
selection process driven by his own stated authenticity-plus-brevity
criterion, not a claim that everything excluded was judged fabricated.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 4** applies to this dossier's own conclusion about the
"~600,000 to ~7,500" gap: the popular framing on both the apologetic side
("brutal 99% filtering proves rigor") and the critical side ("so almost
everything was rejected as fake") is flagged as an oversimplification not
supported by Bukhari's own stated criterion, rather than adopting either
framing uncritically. **Principle 1** is the broader point this answer
exists to serve: knowing that Bukhari contains mawquf/maqtu'/mu'allaq
material alongside marfu' hadith is a precondition for correctly weighing
any specific "Sahih al-Bukhari" citation elsewhere in this project — a
citation's category (is it even claiming to be the Prophet's own words?)
should be checked before its authenticity is debated.
