# 2.1 — The creation-in-seven-days hadith: Ka'b al-Ahbar, Abu Hurairah, and the Qur'an

## Answer

The hadith is authentically recorded in Sahih Muslim (no. 2789), narrated
by Abu Hurairah, stating Allah created the clay/soil on Saturday, the
mountains on Sunday, the trees on Monday, "the things entailing labour"
on Tuesday, light on Wednesday, animals on Thursday, and Adam after 'Asr
on Friday — seven distinct creative acts across seven days, with no
"day of rest." Its inclusion in Sahih Muslim, on its own, is **not**
sufficient grounds to treat it as a Prophetic statement, for two
independent reasons that both predate this project and were raised by
mainstream Sunni hadith critics themselves — this is not a Shia
reinterpretation of the report, it is the internal Sunni critical
tradition on it:

1. **Attribution problem (the report may not even be a hadith of the
   Prophet ﷺ at all).** Imam al-Bukhari, in his own *Tarikh al-Kabir*
   (1:413), and separately Yahya ibn Ma'in, held that Abu Hurairah
   related this from **Ka'b al-Ahbar** — a Jewish convert to Islam and a
   well-known conduit for Isra'iliyyat (transmitted Judeo-Christian
   material) — and not from the Prophet ﷺ. That makes the report *mawquf*
   (a companion's own statement, mixed up with Ka'b's material) rather
   than *marfu'* (attributed to the Prophet). Ibn Kathir summarized the
   position starkly: **"This report is among the *gharaib* [anomalies] of
   Sahih Muslim; Bukhari and others discussed it and held it to be the
   speech of Ka'b al-Ahbar."** Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim later cited
   this view approvingly.
2. **A separate, harder chain-of-transmission problem.** Ibn al-Madini
   identified a version of this report's chain running through
   **Ibrahim ibn Abi Yahya**, a narrator independently accused of
   forging narrations and considered unreliable in his own right — a
   distinct defect from the Ka'b al-Ahbar attribution question.

Against this, the hadith's content also does not sit comfortably next to
the Qur'an's own repeated statement that the heavens and earth were
created in **six** days (7:54, 10:3, 11:7, 25:59, 32:4, 50:38), with no
mention of a seventh day of further creation beyond that. Defenders (see
Al-Mu'allimi al-Yamani and al-Albani, both quoted verbatim already in
Chapter 2 §2.1) argue the "seven days" describe a separate, later stage
of terrestrial habitability rather than the six days of the heavens-and-
earth's creation — a reconciliation that is possible, but only necessary
*if* the report is accepted as authentic in the first place, which is
exactly what is contested above.

**Bottom line, applying Methodology Principle 2 directly:** "It's in
Sahih Muslim" is not sufficient reason to accept this report as a
statement of the Prophet ﷺ. Bukhari — the more stringent of the two
collectors on this exact point — and Ibn Kathir both treat it as
Ka'b al-Ahbar's material, not the Prophet's; a second, independent chain
defect (Ibrahim ibn Abi Yahya) exists on top of that. Abu Hurairah's
overall reputation as the most prolific narrator in the Sunni corpus does
not settle this specific report's authenticity — which is precisely the
point this question was raised to test.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 2** (no narrator is exempt from scrutiny by fame) is the
direct test case here: Abu Hurairah's status as the most prolific
narrator in the Sunni corpus is not treated as a reason to accept this
specific report; the report is instead weighed on its own chain and
content, exactly as the methodology requires. **Principle 1** (rijal
grading is not self-authenticating in either direction) also applies:
this dossier does not simply defer to "Sahih Muslim included it" (one
direction), but it likewise does not simply defer to "Bukhari and Ibn
Kathir rejected it" as an automatic final word either — both positions
are reported with their reasoning so a reader can judge the reasoning,
not just the verdict.
