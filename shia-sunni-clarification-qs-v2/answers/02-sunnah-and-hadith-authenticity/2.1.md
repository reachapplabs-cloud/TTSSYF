## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

The hadith is authentically recorded in Sahih Muslim, commonly numbered
2789 [R1], narrated by Abu Hurairah: Allah created the clay/soil on
Saturday, the mountains on Sunday, the trees on Monday, "the things
entailing labour" on Tuesday, light on Wednesday, animals on Thursday,
and Adam after 'Asr on Friday, seven distinct creative acts across seven
days, with no day of rest mentioned. Its presence in Sahih Muslim is, on
its own, not sufficient grounds to treat it as a Prophetic statement,
for two independent reasons that both predate this project and were
raised by mainstream Sunni hadith critics themselves. This is not a
Shia reinterpretation of the report, it is the internal Sunni critical
tradition on it.

**Attribution problem, whether this is even a hadith of the Prophet at
all.** Imam al-Bukhari himself, in his *Tarikh al-Kabir* [R2], and
separately Yahya ibn Ma'in, held that Abu Hurairah related this from
Ka'b al-Ahbar, a Jewish convert to Islam and a well-known conduit for
Isra'iliyyat (transmitted Judeo-Christian material), not from the
Prophet. That would make the report *mawquf* (a companion's own
statement, entangled with Ka'b's material) rather than *marfu'*
(attributed to the Prophet). Ibn Kathir's verdict was stark: this
report is among the *gharaib* (anomalies) of Sahih Muslim, and Bukhari
and others held it to be the speech of Ka'b al-Ahbar [R3]. Ibn Taymiyyah
[R5] and Ibn al-Qayyim later cited this view approvingly.

**A separate, harder chain defect.** Ibn al-Madini identified a version
of this report's chain running through Ibrahim ibn Abi Yahya, a narrator
independently accused of forging narrations [R4], a distinct problem
from the Ka'b al-Ahbar attribution question above.

Against this, the hadith's content also sits uneasily beside the
Qur'an's own repeated statement that the heavens and earth were created
in six days [R8], with no seventh day of further creation mentioned.
Defenders, Al-Mu'allimi al-Yamani [R6] and al-Albani [R7] both argue,
quoted verbatim elsewhere in this compilation, that the "seven days"
describe a separate, later stage of terrestrial habitability rather
than the six days of the heavens and earth's own creation. That
reconciliation is possible, but it is only necessary *if* the report is
accepted as Prophetic speech in the first place, which is exactly what
Bukhari [R2] and Ibn Kathir [R3] dispute on chain grounds, separately
from the content question entirely.

### The Shia position, after this research

This is one of the stronger, more precisely correct claims in this whole
compilation, and it does not need to be overstated to land. "It's in
Sahih Muslim" [R1] is not sufficient reason to accept this report as the
Prophet's own statement: Bukhari, the more stringent of the two
collectors on this exact point [R2], and Ibn Kathir [R3] both hold it to
be Ka'b al-Ahbar's material, not the Prophet's, and a second,
independent chain defect (Ibrahim ibn Abi Yahya) [R4] sits on top of
that. Abu Hurairah's overall reputation as the most prolific narrator in
the Sunni corpus does not settle this specific report's authenticity,
which is precisely what Methodology Principle 2 predicts and precisely
why this was chosen as its test case.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

The honest and, in this case, genuinely strong Sunni response is not to
defend this report as Prophetic speech, it is to point out that
mainstream Sunni hadith criticism already treats it as likely
non-Prophetic Isra'iliyyat via Ka'b al-Ahbar, by Bukhari's own
independent judgment [R2], not a concession forced by outside pressure.
In other words, the sharpest form of this challenge is already conceded
inside the Sunni critical tradition itself, and has been for centuries;
a report sitting in Sahih Muslim is not the same thing as Sahih Muslim's
own compiler asserting every included report is Prophetic in origin, a
distinction classical Sunni hadith science itself draws explicitly. The
compatibility arguments (Mu'allimi [R6], Albani [R7]) exist for readers
who do treat the report as Prophetic despite the attribution question,
they are a secondary line of defense, not the primary one, and
conflating "some scholars defended it" with "mainstream Sunni
scholarship affirms it" overstates the position more than most careful
Sunni scholars would.

### What would resolve this

An independent second pass with working access to sunnah.com, to confirm
the exact hadith number and permalink and read the Arabic isnad text
directly, and to a primary edition of Ibn Kathir's and Ibn al-Madini's
own works rather than relying on a secondary summary of them.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 2** is the direct test case: Abu Hurairah's status as the
most prolific narrator is not treated as a reason to accept this report;
it is weighed on its own chain and content. **Principle 1** applies too:
this dossier does not simply defer to "Sahih Muslim included it," but it
equally does not simply defer to "Bukhari and Ibn Kathir rejected it" as
an automatic final word, both positions are reported with their
reasoning so the reasoning can be judged, not just the verdict.
