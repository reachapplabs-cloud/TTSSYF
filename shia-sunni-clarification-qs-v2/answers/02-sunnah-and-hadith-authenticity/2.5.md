## Answer

See [Chapter 2, §2.5](../../chapters/02-sunnah-and-hadith-authenticity.md)
for the full answer. Summary: the quote — "we do not see the righteous
more false in anything than they are regarding Hadith," with Muslim's
gloss "falsehood flows upon their tongues although they do not intend to
lie" — is attributed to Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan and recorded by Muslim
in his Muqaddimah as the justification for applying isnad-criticism
universally, not as a self-undermining claim. It is treated here as
strong primary-source support for this project's own Principles 1–2, and
as raising a still-open question this project has not resolved: whether
chain-based criticism reliably catches sincere, unintentional inaccuracy
as opposed to deliberate lying.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 1 and Principle 2 directly** — this item is not a case study
this project is applying its own principles *to*; it is a piece of
evidence *for* those principles, sourced from within the classical Sunni
hadith-critical tradition itself. It shows that a major hadith critic
(al-Qattan) and a major collector (Muslim) already recognized that
reputation/piety does not guarantee narrator reliability, which is the
same claim Principle 2 makes. **Principle 4** also applies to how the
"sharper question" in the Answer is framed: this project explicitly
declines to assert a settled verdict on whether isnad-criticism reliably
catches sincere-but-inaccurate narration, logging it as an open question
rather than resolving it either toward "the system works" or "the system
fails," since neither has been established by this session's research.
