## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

The Sunni dating of Qur'an 5:3 to the Day of Arafah rests on a specific,
named report, not a vague tradition. Sahih al-Bukhari records that a Jew
told Umar ibn al-Khattab that if this verse had been revealed to the
Jews, they would have taken that day as a festival; Umar replied that he
knew exactly when and where it was revealed, Friday, at Arafat, during
the Farewell Hajj [R1]. Muslim, al-Tirmidhi, and al-Nasa'i are widely
reported to carry the same or a closely parallel report [R1]. That is a
strong, specific, multiply-attested claim on the Sunni side, not an
undated generality.

The logical challenge the question raises, that "completion of religion"
sitting alongside an unfinished Hajj (Muzdalifah, the stoning, sacrifice,
tawaf al-wida all still ahead) is an odd claim to make mid-ritual, has a
textually grounded Sunni answer, not only an ad hoc one. Classical tafsir
treats "completion of religion" as completion of core legislation and
creed, not the mechanical completion of that specific year's Hajj
itinerary: al-Tabari and al-Zamakhshari are reported to hold that no
further ritual or legal ruling was revealed after this verse, while
al-Qurtubi is reported to qualify that some legal, but no ritual,
injunctions still followed [R2]. Separately, the well-known hadith "al-Hajju
'Arafah" ("Hajj is Arafah") establishes standing at Arafah as the
pilgrimage's defining, indispensable pillar, with the remaining rites
understood as required completions of an already-valid Hajj rather than
additional foundational legislation [R3]. Read together, these give a
real answer to the "how can the pillar be done" framing: the argument is
that the doctrinal core was sealed, not that the physical rite-schedule
for that year was finished, and those are explicitly treated as two
different kinds of completeness in this literature, this dossier just
has not independently opened Tabari's or Qurtubi's primary text to
confirm the wording carries that distinction as cleanly as the secondary
characterization suggests.

The strongest form of the Shia case is not really a logical objection to
the Sunni account at all, it is a competing factual claim that removes
the problem by relocating the event. Mainstream Shia scholarship dates
the revelation of 5:3 to Ghadir Khumm, three days later on 18 Dhu
al-Hijjah, on the return journey after every Hajj rite, including tawaf
al-wida, was already finished, immediately after the Prophet's
declaration concerning Ali [R4]. Under that account the "incomplete Hajj"
tension the question raises simply does not arise, because by this
dating nothing was still underway when the verse came down. That is a
materially different, and cleaner, resolution than merely asserting the
Sunni timing is philosophically incoherent.

What keeps this from being a one-sided case, and is a direct instance of
this project's own rijal-scrutiny principle, is that Shia hadith
criticism does not treat its own tradition's Arafah-dating report
uncritically either. Al-Kafi carries a narration, via a narrator named
Ja'far al-Khuza'i, dating the verse to Arafah, matching the Sunni
account rather than the Ghadir account; Shia rijal scholars themselves,
al-Majlisi in Bihar al-Anwar and al-Khoei in Mu'jam Rijal al-Hadith, are
reported to grade this narration weak because the narrator is unknown
and unauthenticated [R5]. That is the same discipline this project
applies to Sunni-side grading (Principle 1) turned symmetrically on a
Shia primary source, an internal correction the Shia scholarly tradition
made on its own terms, not one imposed from outside it. Separately,
al-Tabari's tafsir is reported to present, and reject, competing
traditions attributed to Ibn Abbas connecting the verse to Ghadir, taking
his own explicit verdict against them [R6], though this specific claim is
the weakest-sourced item in this dossier and needs the most caution.

### The Shia position, after this research

The case does not need to prove the Arafah dating is self-contradictory
on its face, mainstream Shia scholarship simply holds it is the wrong
date: the verse came down at Ghadir Khumm on 18 Dhu al-Hijjah, after
Hajj had fully concluded and the caravan was already on its way home,
immediately following the Prophet's declaration about Ali, which is what
"completed my favor upon you" is read as referring to [R4]. On this
account the "how can Islam be complete before Hajj is finished" question
dissolves rather than needing an answer, because nothing was unfinished
by the time of the actual revelation. The tradition's own rejection of
the Al-Kafi Arafah-dating report as weak [R5] is presented here as a
strength, not a liability: it shows Shia rijal criticism catching an
internally inconvenient narration rather than waving it through because
it happened to exist in a major collection.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

The Arafah dating is not a loose claim, it traces to a specific,
multiply-attested report naming Umar as the source, carried in Bukhari
and reported to be corroborated in Muslim, al-Tirmidhi, and al-Nasa'i
[R1]. The "religion complete, Hajj unfinished" tension is answered by
distinguishing what kind of completeness the verse claims: the doctrinal
and legislative core of Islam, per the reported Tabari/Zamakhshari/Qurtubi
readings [R2], not the literal completion of that year's pilgrimage
schedule, and Arafah is independently established elsewhere in the hadith
corpus as the defining pillar of Hajj, with the remaining rites required
completions rather than additional core legislation [R3]. On this
reading, the objection assumes a definition of "complete" the verse was
never claiming in the first place.

Tirmidhi, and Nasa'i parallels, to confirm the wording exactly rather

confirm the weak grading of the Al-Kafi Arafah report rather than relying
on a search-surfaced Q&A page summarizing it.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 1** applies directly and symmetrically here: this dossier
does not accept the Sunni Bukhari 45 report as automatically settling
the question just because it is in Bukhari, and it applies the identical
scrutiny to the Shia side by foregrounding that Shia rijal criticism
itself graded the Al-Kafi Arafah-dating report weak, rather than only
reporting the Shia tradition's preferred Ghadir-dating conclusion.
**Principle 4** applies to the question's own framing: the "how can Islam
be complete before Hajj is finished" objection is treated as a claim to
research rather than a settled contradiction, and the research found
that the strongest Shia answer to it is not a philosophical rebuttal at
all but a factual relocation of the event to a date after Hajj had
concluded, which is a materially different and more specific claim than
the question as originally posed implies.
