## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

**The exact phrase "Allah has written for me to say" could not be traced.**
This session's research did not locate any primary hadith, sermon, or
speech attributed to Umar using this specific wording. That absence is
reported plainly rather than papered over, this is either a loose
paraphrase or composite of other material, a reference to a source this
session simply could not find, or a claim needing the contributor's
original source (video or otherwise) before it can be properly checked.
Three genuine, well-documented parallels exist, though, and are worth
laying out honestly as the likely referents, without claiming any of them
is confirmed as the actual source of the quoted phrase.

**Parallel 1: the "muhaddath" hadith.** A hadith recorded in Sahih
al-Bukhari has the Prophet ﷺ say that among nations before Islam there
were people who were divinely inspired without being prophets, and that
if such a person existed in his own nation, it would be Umar [R1]. A
related report in Jami' al-Tirmidhi has the Prophet ﷺ say more directly
that Allah has placed truth upon Umar's tongue and in his heart [R1].
Classical commentators, per Ibn Hajar, differ on what *muhaddath* means
precisely, understood, inspired, or spoken to by angels, with the
"spoken to by angels" reading being the majority position [R1]. This
hadith explicitly frames the phenomenon as something attested even among
non-prophets in prior nations, it is textually careful not to claim
prophetic revelation for Umar.

**Parallel 2: the stoning-verse speech.** Umar is recorded, in a report
agreed upon by Bukhari and Muslim, addressing the people and saying that
Allah sent Muhammad ﷺ with the truth and revealed the Book to him, and
that the verse of stoning was part of what was revealed; that they
recited it, memorized it, and understood it; that the Prophet ﷺ enforced
it as law and it continued to be enforced after his death; and that he
feared later generations might deny an obligation Allah had sent down
because its wording was no longer found in the compiled Qur'an [R2]. This
is a much closer textual match in spirit to "Allah has written," Umar is
here explicitly asserting the existence and continued legal force of a
piece of divine revelation outside the compiled text, on his own
authority as its witness.

**Parallel 3: the Saqifah "Allah opened his chest" framing.** In Umar's
own later recollection of events at Saqifah, recorded in Sahih
al-Bukhari, he describes recognizing that Abu Bakr's resolve on the
succession question was rooted in truth, using language to the effect
that Allah had opened Abu Bakr's chest to the matter [R3]. This is framed
as Umar's own retrospective judgment about Abu Bakr, not a claim about
himself, and is a weaker match to the quoted phrase, but is included here
because it is the closest primary material connecting Umar's own words to
language of divinely-guided succession specifically.

**A conflation worth surfacing in the original question itself.** The
chapter's framing links "claiming to be a khulafa rashideen" to "claiming
divine sanction... despite no divine appointment." These are actually two
separate Sunni claims that should not be treated as one: being counted
among the *Khulafa Rashidun* is a retrospective honorific tied to method
of selection and conduct, not a claim that any of the four received
personal revelation appointing them; and the *muhaddath* hadith [R1]
claims something narrower still, occasional inspired truthfulness, not
prophetic appointment. Sunni theology's own account of how Umar became
Caliph rests on Abu Bakr's nomination and communal acceptance (shura), not
on a claim of individual revelation the way Muhammad's ﷺ prophethood is
claimed. That said, this technical distinction does not fully dissolve
the substantive concern the question is pointing at: the stoning-verse
speech [R2] genuinely does deploy quasi-scriptural authority, asserting
a piece of revelation exists and must be legally binding despite being
absent from the compiled Qur'an, entirely independent of whether the
specific quoted phrase in the question is ever confirmed.

### The Shia position, after this research

Even without the exact quoted phrase confirmed, the substantive concern
stands on genuinely attested material: the stoning-verse speech [R2] has
Umar asserting, on his own recollection alone, that legally binding
revealed text existed outside the compiled Mushaf and must continue to
govern law, a striking claim about extra-textual religious authority
resting entirely on one man's testimony. Combined with the muhaddath
hadith's elevation of Umar to a category of divinely-inspired truthfulness
[R1], and consistent with this project's Principle 5 findings elsewhere
in this compilation (§4.13) that most hadith praising Umar's stature is
dated by Shia scholarship to circulation during the Umayyad period rather
than Umar's own lifetime, the pattern the question is gesturing at, Umar's
authority being dressed in quasi-revelatory language, has real textual
grounding even if this specific quoted sentence does not.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

On its own careful terms, Sunni theology does not actually claim Umar
received personal revelation appointing him Caliph, his legitimacy rests
on nomination and communal acceptance, and the muhaddath hadith [R1] is
explicit that it describes a phenomenon attested among non-prophets, not
prophetic revelation, so "claiming divine sanction... despite no divine
appointment" mischaracterizes what mainstream Sunni sources actually
assert about him even by their own account. The stoning-verse speech [R2]
is better read, on this account, as Umar preserving a legal ruling he
personally witnessed being revealed and enforced by the Prophet ﷺ himself,
a claim about eyewitness memory of what the Prophet ﷺ did, not a claim
that Umar himself possessed independent access to revelation.

### What would resolve this

Most urgently, the contributor supplying the actual source, video,
book, or specific speech, for the "Allah has written for me to say"
quotation, since without it this dossier can only offer parallels rather
than a direct verification. Beyond that: direct access to Sahih
al-Bukhari 6829 and 7323 and Sahih Muslim 1691 for the stoning-verse
speech's exact wording and isnad [R2]; direct access to Jami' al-Tirmidhi
3682 for the muhaddath hadith's chain and classical commentary on what
*muhaddath* means [R1]; and direct access to Sahih al-Bukhari's Saqifah
narrations for the exact wording of Umar's "Allah opened his chest"
recollection about Abu Bakr [R3].

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 1** is applied to the muhaddath hadith and the stoning-verse
speech alike, both are treated as reports to weigh on their own textual
content and implications, not simply accepted as praiseworthy because
they appear in Bukhari and Muslim, or dismissed because they concern a
figure this project's overall subject matter treats critically. **Principle
4** is applied to the original question's own framing, the conflation of
"Khulafa Rashidun" with "divine appointment" is surfaced explicitly as an
imprecision in how the question itself is posed, consistent with this
project's practice of checking a contributor's own framing rather than
only checking the sources it cites. **Principle 3** is relevant in a
narrow sense, the unconfirmed quoted phrase is treated as an open gap to
flag rather than silently smoothed over by substituting a plausible-
sounding parallel in its place.
