### Weighing it — aql, evidence & sources

This question has two genuinely separate parts, and they should not be
answered as one. Part one: what actually happened in the succession to
Umar, the six-member shura and Uthman's election. Part two: does this
specific hadith's text use "fulan" for Ali, and if so, what does that
mean.

**Part one is not seriously in dispute.** The outline the source gives
is the standard, widely attested account across Sunni historical and
hadith literature [R1], not a fringe reconstruction: after Umar was
stabbed by Abu Lu'lu'a Firuz, he named a six-member consultative council
(Uthman, Ali, Talha, Zubayr, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas)
to choose the next caliph from among themselves. Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf,
after consulting the community, put the same condition to both Ali and
Uthman: would they rule by the Book of Allah, the Sunnah of the Prophet,
and the precedent (*sira*) of the two sheikhs before them, Abu Bakr and
Umar. Ali is reported to have answered that he would do his best
according to his own knowledge and judgment, declining to bind himself
unconditionally to following Abu Bakr and Umar's precedent in every
particular; Uthman agreed without that qualification, and Abd al-Rahman
gave him the pledge. This is taught, not hidden, in mainstream Sunni
retellings of how Uthman became caliph, and Ali's conditional answer is
itself usually presented as a mark of his integrity, not suppressed as
an embarrassment. Ali's own election after Uthman's assassination, and
the revolt that followed (leading into the First Fitna, covered
elsewhere in this compilation), is likewise standard, undisputed
history.

**Part two is where honesty requires a flat statement of a limit:** I
cannot confirm from memory, with the confidence this project requires,
whether the specific published text at "Sahih al-Bukhari, Volume 8,
Book 82, Hadith 817" (the old Muhsin Khan/USC-MSA numbering the source
links to [R4]) contains the literal word *fulan* at any point, still
less that it does so in reference to Ali specifically rather than to
some other unnamed figure elsewhere in what is a very long, multi-part
narration that Bukhari places across several chapters of his
collection. This session cannot reach sunnah.com or any primary hadith
database directly (a standing, confirmed limitation for the whole
project, not new to this question) to check the actual wording [R2].
Asserting the *fulan* claim as confirmed, or asserting it as false,
would both be exactly the kind of face-value move this project's own
methodology exists to prevent. Marked as a specific, actionable open
item below, not glossed over.

**What can be said honestly about Bukhari's use of *fulan* in general,
independent of this specific instance:** it is a real, documented
editorial practice across hadith literature generally, not unique to
Bukhari or to Ali-related material [R3]. Classical scholars record more
than one reason for it: a narrator sometimes genuinely did not know or
had not independently verified a name and reported the hadith exactly
as heard rather than fill the gap with a guess, which classical hadith
critics treat as a mark of transmission scrupulousness, not
concealment; in other cases a narrator avoided naming a still-living
person in an unflattering context out of social caution. Separately,
classical commentary, Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani's *Fath al-Bari* being the
standard example [R3], routinely cross-references parallel narrations
specifically to identify who an unnamed *fulan* was elsewhere in the
text, which is itself normal scholarly practice for resolving such
cases, not evidence either for or against concealment in any single
instance. So the mere fact that *fulan* appears somewhere in the hadith
corpus proves nothing by itself, in either direction; per Principle 1,
each instance needs its own check, which is exactly why this is logged
as a test case rather than resolved by generalization.

### The Shia position, after this research

The strongest form of this question isn't "Bukhari hides Ali's name,
proven," it's narrower and more defensible: *if* this specific hadith's
text does use *fulan* at the point the source claims [R2], that would
sit inside a real, separately documented pattern, that unnamed
placeholders do appear in hadith literature touching politically
sensitive material from the earliest, most contested period of Islamic
history, the exact period this succession narrative comes from [R1].
That pattern is worth taking seriously as a reason to check this
specific claim carefully, not as a reason to assume it's already
settled. What weakens the claim as currently stated is that the
identification of the unnamed party as Ali is, on the record available
to this project, the contributor's own inference from "similar ahadith
and exegesis," not a citation of a specific classical commentator
making that identification by name. A stronger version of this argument
would name that commentator and passage directly, the way Ibn Hajar's
own cross-referencing method [R3] would require.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

Ali's central role in the shura, his conditional answer, and Uthman's
election are not hidden anywhere in mainstream Sunni retelling, they
are the standard account, taught openly [R1], and Ali's answer is
typically presented as principled rather than embarrassing. If the
specific narration at this citation does use *fulan* in one clause, the
standard Sunni scholarly response to that is exactly what Ibn Hajar and
others routinely do across the corpus [R3]: cross-reference parallel
chains and name the unnamed party in commentary, which is transparency
achieved through the tradition's own cross-referencing method, not
concealment, since the identity would not actually be hidden across the
tradition taken as a whole, only unnamed in one narrator's particular
telling. Whether that is in fact what happened here specifically still
depends on checking the actual text and its classical commentary, which
this dossier has not yet done.

### What would resolve this

The single concrete next step: pull the actual Arabic and a standard
English translation of this hadith at the cited location (or its modern
sunnah.com-numbered equivalent, since the old Khan/USC-MSA numbering
this source cites does not map one-to-one onto current reference
numbers), read the full text directly rather than from memory, and check
specifically (a) whether *fulan* appears at all, (b) if so, in which
clause and referring to whom, and (c) whether Ibn Hajar's *Fath al-Bari*
or another classical commentary on this hadith identifies that party by
name. This is flagged in `METHODOLOGY.md` open item #7 and stays open
until that direct check happens.

## Methodology Applied

- **Principle 3** (concealment patterns) is the question this dossier
  exists to test, and is applied here by refusing to treat the *fulan*
  claim (R2) as either confirmed or dismissed without checking the
  actual text, exactly the posture the principle calls for.
- **Principle 1** (rijal/reliability grading is not self-authenticating
  in either direction) is applied by extension: the general fact that
  *fulan* appears somewhere in hadith literature (R3) is treated as
  neutral until the specific instance is checked, refusing to read it
  as automatic evidence of either concealment or of innocent
  transmission practice.
