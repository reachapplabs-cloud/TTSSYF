## Answer

The source message bundles three distinct things without fully spelling
out the connection between them: Khutbah ash-Shiqshiqiyyah, Qur'an
24:55, and an episode involving Ali's advice to Umar over a proposed
military expedition. Each is weighed separately below rather than
assumed to form one argument.

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

**Khutbah ash-Shiqshiqiyyah** is Sermon 3 of *Nahj al-Balagha*, the
collected sermons and sayings of Ali compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi in
the tenth century [R1]. In it, Ali expresses sharp, grief-toned language
about being passed over for the succession he believed was rightfully
his, using the image of the caliphate as something he set aside "as one
sets aside a she-camel's froth," and describing having to swallow the
affront "with a throat full of thorns" until circumstances eventually
forced him to accept the caliphate himself, after Uthman's death.
Content-wise this is the single clearest primary statement in the whole
tradition of Ali's own view of the succession.

**Its authenticity is a genuine, long-standing scholarly question, not
only a Sunni objection.** *Nahj al-Balagha* was compiled centuries after
Ali's death as a literary anthology, and unlike a hadith collection its
entries do not each carry a full documented isnad. Some later scholars,
Shia and Sunni alike, have raised the question of how much of the
sermon's exact wording reflects verbatim transmission versus later
literary compilation from older, looser sources [R2]. This does not
mean the sermon's content is fabricated wholesale, most classical
treatments accept some real historical core of Ali's grievance being
transmitted, but the exact wording carrying the emotional and rhetorical
weight this sermon is usually cited for is a separate, harder question
from whether Ali held some form of this grievance at all.

**Qur'an 24:55**, the "verse of succession" (*ayat al-istikhlaf*),
promises that Allah will grant succession and authority on earth to
believers who do righteous deeds, as He granted it to those before
them [R3]. The verse names no specific individual. In practice, Sunni
exegesis generally reads it as a general promise fulfilled communally
through the early Muslim community's political success under the
Rashidun order broadly, while some Shia argument pairs the verse's
general succession-promise with more specific evidence elsewhere
(Ghadeer Khumm, covered at §6.4) to argue for who that succession was
meant to specifically fall to. The source material doesn't spell out
which specific argument is being invoked here, so this is offered as
general context for how the verse commonly functions in this debate,
not a resolution of a specific claim the source makes.

**Ali's advice to Umar against personally leading a military
expedition** is a commonly cited historical episode [R4]: Ali reportedly
counseled Umar not to lead a campaign in person, on the reasoning that
if the caliph himself were killed or defeated it would collapse the
whole community's position, and that a commander should be sent instead.
This episode, if accurately remembered here, is worth noting for a
reason that cuts against a simple reading of this whole question: it
shows Ali continuing to actively advise and cooperate with Umar's
administration, which complicates any picture of total estrangement
between them, whatever grief Ali privately held per the sermon above.

### The Shia position, after this research

The sermon's content, regardless of exactly how much of its precise
wording is verbatim, is real primary evidence that Ali himself held and
expressed a grievance about being passed over, not something invented
retrospectively by later Shia tradition [R1]. Paired with 24:55's
general succession-promise and, more specifically, the Ghadeer Khumm
material covered separately in this compilation, the cumulative Shia
case is that succession was meant to follow explicit designation rather
than open communal selection, and Ali's own sermon is the clearest
primary voice for how he understood what had happened to him.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

Even granting the sermon's content as reflecting real grief, that is not
by itself evidence that Ali considered the succession process invalid
or illegitimate: a person can feel passed over without concluding the
process that passed him over was wrong, and Ali's continued practical
cooperation and advisory role under Umar [R4] is real evidence pointing
that way. The authenticity question around *Nahj al-Balagha*'s exact
wording [R2] is also a genuine methodological caution worth taking
seriously before treating any single phrase from the sermon as Ali's
precisely verified words. And 24:55's general, unnamed promise of
succession to righteous believers is the more natural reading of the
verse on its own terms, without importing a specific-individual claim
the text itself doesn't make.

### What would resolve this

Direct access to *Nahj al-Balagha*'s manuscript history and the
classical scholarly discussion of Sermon 3's authenticity, to state the
"how much is verbatim" question with actual sourcing rather than this
dossier's general characterization; direct access to classical tafsir
on 24:55 to confirm how it's actually used on each side rather than this
dossier's reconstruction; and a primary source for the Umar-expedition
episode to confirm it accurately and check its dating relative to other
events in this period.
