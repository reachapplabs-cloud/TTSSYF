## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

**Sermon 3** is Khutbah ash-Shiqshiqiyyah, already treated in full at
[§6.1](6.1.md), not repeated here, this entry only adds the direct
source link and doesn't change that analysis.

**Sermon 193 is worth a correction before anything else:** it is widely
known as *Khutbat al-Muttaqin* (the Sermon of the God-Fearing),
delivered, per tradition, in response to a companion named Hammam
asking Ali to describe the character of the pious [R1]. It is one of
the longest and most celebrated pieces in *Nahj al-Balagha*, but its
actual content is an ethical and spiritual discourse describing the
qualities, conduct, and inner life of righteous believers, it is not,
in its primary content, an argument about the succession or the
khilafah dispute at all. Grouping it alongside Sermon 3 as if both are
succession texts overstates what Sermon 193 itself actually argues.

**Why it might still belong in this conversation:** the more honest
connection is implicit rather than stated, a character-based argument
for who was most qualified to lead: a sermon this widely regarded for
its moral and spiritual depth is sometimes invoked to make the case that
Ali's understanding of piety and justice, on display in Sermon 193
itself, is evidence for his qualification to have led, rather than
Sermon 193 containing any direct claim about the succession dispute.
That is a real argument some make, but it should be stated as an
inference being drawn from the sermon's existence and reputation, not
as something the sermon's text itself argues.

**On the contributor's own reflection** ("beautiful read, even more so
in Arabic," and the earlier remark about Sermon 3 evoking "what we had
to endure after the cheat swayed power"): per Principle 4, this is
preserved as the contributor's personal reaction and reflection, not a
citation or an argument requiring a rebuttal in this dossier.

### The Shia position, after this research

The strongest form of this pairing doesn't claim Sermon 193 is directly
about the succession, it uses it as character evidence: a sermon this
widely regarded for its moral and spiritual insight into piety is
offered as a window into the caliber of leadership the Shia case argues
was set aside, strengthening the more direct succession argument made
via Sermon 3 and the Ghadeer Khumm material (§6.4) rather than
duplicating it.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

Sermon 193's genuine subject matter is piety and ethical conduct, not
the khilafah dispute, and it is worth being precise about that rather
than letting its pairing with Sermon 3 imply otherwise. A sermon's
eloquence and moral depth, real as they may be, is not itself an
argument about who should have succeeded the Prophet; using it that way
is an inference layered on top of the text, not something the sermon
itself claims, and the strength of that inference should be judged
separately from the sermon's genuine literary and spiritual standing,
which is not in dispute.

### What would resolve this

Direct access to *Nahj al-Balagha* to confirm Sermon 193's full text and
its traditional framing (the Hammam narrative), and to check whether any
classical commentary reads it as bearing on the succession question
directly rather than only as general character evidence, as this
dossier currently assumes without a specific citation for that use.
