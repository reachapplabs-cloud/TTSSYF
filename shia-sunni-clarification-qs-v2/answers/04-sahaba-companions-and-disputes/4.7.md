# 4.7 — Which companion (identified: Talhah ibn Ubaydullah) was "already planning" after the Prophet's ﷺ death?

## Answer

The companion is **Talhah ibn Ubaydullah**, per a supplied document
("Comprehensive Historical Analysis of Talhah ibn Ubaydullah," PDF,
2026-08-28) — not per the original chat messages, which never named him.
The identification fits the original description closely: a companion
reportedly "already planning his actions" before the Prophet's ﷺ death
(the document's claim that he voiced intent to marry Aisha should the
Prophet die), and reportedly involved in turning against Uthman and then
waging war under the banner of avenging him (the document's account of
Talhah blocking water to the besieged Uthman, then allying with Aisha and
Al-Zubayr against Caliph Ali under an Uthman-avenging banner).

**This project has not independently verified the document's citations.**
Per `METHODOLOGY.md` Principle 4, a supplied secondary document is
context to research, not fact to adopt — this Answer records what the
document claims and how it maps onto the original question, not an
independent confirmation that every cited book says exactly what the
document says it does. The document is extensively and specifically
cited (particular volumes, pages, and named books for nearly every claim
— see the chapter section for the full list), which is a meaningfully
higher evidentiary bar than an unsourced claim, but citation density is
not the same as this project having opened those primary sources itself.

**What is unusually strong here, even before independent verification:**
the document cites the identification of Talhah as the subject of Qur'an
33:53's revelation to a named, specific hadith transmission (Abu Bakr ibn
Muhammad ibn 'Amr ibn Hazm, via Ibn Sa'd's *Tabaqat*) and to six
independent tafsir works reaching the same conclusion (al-Tabari,
al-Suyuti, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan) — a
genuinely broad tafsir consensus on that one narrow point, if the
citations hold up, though this project has not opened any of them
directly to confirm the quoted wording.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 4** is the operative principle for this entire dossier: the
supplied document is treated as context supplied by the project
(functionally the same category as the two contributors' own raw
messages), not as an authority to defer to simply because it is dense
with citations. **Principle 1** will apply once individual citations are
checked — several of the document's own claims turn on rijal-adjacent
questions (e.g., whether the marriage-intent chains are *da'if* or
*sahih* is itself disputed between the Sunni and Shia framings the
document describes), and this project should not adopt either verdict
without the same scrutiny given everywhere else in this project.
