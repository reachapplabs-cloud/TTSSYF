## Answer

### Weighing it: aql, evidence and sources

The source material's own citation chain checks out on its base facts,
but its logical step from those facts to "therefore 'and his companions'
is bid'ah" does not follow as cleanly as presented, and that gap is
worth being precise about rather than glossing over in either direction.

**The base facts are solid.** The Prophet's ﷺ own taught formula for
Salawat, given when companions asked how to fulfill Qur'an 33:56, is
recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari (hadith 6357) and Sahih Muslim, narrated via
Ka'b ibn Ujrah: "O Allah, send Your blessings on Muhammad and on the
family of Muhammad, as You sent Your blessings on Ibrahim and on the
family of Ibrahim" [R1]. This wording, known as Durood Ibrahimi, does not
mention companions. Separately, a hadith reported in multiple classical
collections, including Ibn Hajar al-Haytami's Sawa'iq al-Muhriqah,
records the Prophet ﷺ prohibiting the "clipped" or "mutilated" (*batra*)
Salawat, defined as saying "O Allah, send blessings on Muhammad" and
stopping there, instead of continuing "...and the family of Muhammad";
al-Shafi'i is reported to have drawn the conclusion from this that Allah
will not accept the prayer of one who omits blessing the Prophet's ﷺ
family [R2].

**Where the source material's argument overreaches:** the *batra*
prohibition, as reported, is specifically about *truncating* the taught
formula, stopping short before reaching "and the family of Muhammad." It
is not, on the evidence gathered here, a reported prohibition on
*extending* the formula further with additional content. Those are
logically different acts: omitting a required element the Prophet ﷺ
named, versus adding an element he did not name but also did not
explicitly forbid adding. The source material's framing, that the
Prophet's ﷺ silence on companions in this one specific answer to one
specific question functions as an implicit, sealed prohibition on ever
mentioning them in any Salawat formula, is an inference, not something
the *batra* hadith itself states. This project should be honest that this
step is the weakest link in the source material's own case, not
something to pass over because the surrounding citations are accurate.

**Real complicating evidence on the "sealed exclusivity" premise
specifically:** other reported Prophetic-taught Salawat variants exist
that extend beyond the bare Ibrahimi formula in different directions,
including a version instructing blessings on Muhammad ﷺ, "his wives, and
his offspring" [R3]. If the Prophet ﷺ himself is reported to have taught
more than one wording across different occasions, some naming only Aal,
some extending to wives and offspring, then the premise that any one
specific answer functions as a permanently sealed, exhaustive formula
closed to any addition becomes harder to sustain as an absolute rule,
though this project has not independently verified the authenticity or
precise chains of the wives-and-offspring variant, and flags it at lower
confidence than the core Bukhari 6357 wording.

**The "mass media" coinage argument is real methodology, but weaker
evidence than the source material treats it as.** Early Islamic
epigraphy from the relevant period does exist and is well documented:
the Dome of the Rock's inscriptions (72 AH/692 CE) quote the Qur'anic
salawat command itself, "God and His angels shower blessings on the
Prophet, ask blessings on him and salute him with a worthy salutation,"
[R4] but, on the evidence found here, do not carry a full ritual Salawat
formula at all, neither the Aal wording nor an ashab wording. Early
Islamic coinage from the same period centers on the shahada and, after
Abd al-Malik's 696-97 CE reform, brief Qur'anic and creedal phrases [R5],
not extended ritual formulas either. That means the absence of "wa
ashabihi" from 7th-century coins and inscriptions is real, but it sits
alongside an equally real absence of the Aal-only formula in that same
material. The coinage argument, as constructed in the source material,
proves too much if taken at face value, it would equally argue against
the authenticity of "wa Aalihi" by the same absence-of-evidence logic,
which the source material does not intend and which this dossier flags
as the argument's structural weak point rather than adopting it as
presented.

### The Shia position, after this research

The core of the case does not depend on the coinage argument holding up
at full strength, it rests on the specific, textually documented formula
the Prophet ﷺ is reported to have taught in response to a direct
question about how to fulfill Qur'an 33:56 [R1], paired with a real
prohibition against truncating that formula short of naming the family
[R2]. Reading Qur'an 33:56 (a general command) through this specific,
Prophetically-defined method (Sunnah clarifying an unqualified Qur'anic
instruction) is a standard and defensible move in Islamic legal
reasoning, not an invented rule for this argument alone. On this
reading, the burden sits with anyone adding "wa ashabihi" to show a
comparably direct Prophetic instruction for it, not with those who stick
to the taught wording, and if that supporting instruction is not there,
the addition is a later communal practice layered onto, not commanded
within, the ritual formula itself, which is a defensible use of the word
bid'ah even without needing the coinage argument to carry independent
weight.

### The Sunni / Ahl-e-Hadith position, after this research

The taught formula in Bukhari 6357 answers one specific question, how to
fulfill 33:56's obligation in the tashahhud context Ka'b ibn Ujrah asked
about, it is not on the evidence gathered here reported as an exhaustive
or exclusive statement barring every other form of blessing the Prophet
ﷺ ever after. The *batra* prohibition [R2] targets omission of Aal
specifically, not addition of anything beyond it, and that is a real gap
in the bid'ah argument's logic, not a matter of interpretation. Other
reported Prophetic-taught variants extending to wives and offspring [R3]
suggest the Prophet ﷺ himself did not treat a single wording as
permanently fixed and unextendable across every occasion. From this
position, adding "and his companions," expressing love and honor for
those who carried and preserved the faith, is a permissible devotional
extension within the space the Prophet ﷺ left open, not a violation of
an explicit textual boundary he set, and this project did not find a
specific Ahl-e-Hadith fatwa or ruling directly defending "wa ashabihi"
this session, so this remains this dossier's own reconstruction of the
strongest available Sunni/Ahl-e-Hadith position rather than a documented
one.

### What would resolve this

Direct access to Ibn Hajar al-Haytami's Sawa'iq al-Muhriqah to read the
*batra* hadith's exact wording and any surrounding commentary on whether
it addresses additions as well as omissions, rather than the
search-result paraphrase used here; direct access to a primary hadith
source for the "wives and offspring" Salawat variant [R3] to confirm its
chain and authenticity rather than relying on an unattributed search
summary; a direct search of Ahl-e-Hadith/Salafi fatwa literature
(specifically South Asian sources, given the question's own framing) for
an actual ruling on "wa ashabihi," since none was found this session
despite a dedicated search; and direct access to islamic-awareness.org's
full inscription and coinage catalog to build a complete, dated timeline
of what Salawat-related content, if any, appears in 1st-century Islamic
epigraphy, rather than the partial picture assembled here.

  formula naming "Muhammad and the family of Muhammad," without
  mentioning companions. **High confidence**; the hadith number, wording,

  not opened directly this session.
- **R2**: The *batra* ("clipped") Salawat prohibition, reported in
  Sharaf al-Mustafa, al-Sakhawi's al-Qawl al-Badi', and Ibn Hajar
  al-Haytami's Sawa'iq al-Muhriqah, and al-Shafi'i's conclusion that
  Allah will not accept prayer omitting blessings on the Prophet's ﷺ
  family. **Medium confidence**; the existence and general content of
  this hadith and al-Shafi'i's statement are corroborated across
  multiple independent search results, but none of the three named
  primary works was opened directly this session to confirm exact
  wording or chain.
- **R3**: A reported Prophetic-taught Salawat variant extending blessings
  to "Muhammad, his wives, and his offspring." **Low-medium confidence**;
  the wording appeared in search results without a specific, independently
  confirmed hadith-collection citation, and this dossier was not able to
  pin its exact source this session; presented here as a real but
  unconfirmed complicating data point, not as settled fact.
- **R4**: The Dome of the Rock's Arabic inscriptions (72 AH/692 CE),
  quoting the shahada and a paraphrase of Qur'an 33:56's salawat command,
  without a full ritual Salawat formula naming either Aal or ashab.
  **Medium confidence**; the inscription's general content is
  well-documented and consistent across sources, but the specific claim
  that no full ritual formula appears in it is this dossier's own
  reading of the reported inscription content, not a claim independently
  made by a cited source.
- **R5**: Abd al-Malik's coinage, the first complete shahada on an
  Islamic gold coin (c. 72 AH/691-92 CE) and the aniconic, Arabic-only
  reform (76-77 AH/696-97 CE), centered on the shahada and brief creedal
  phrases rather than extended ritual formulas. **Medium confidence**;
  consistent across multiple search results and a named academic source
  (Bacharach's "Signs of Sovereignty"), though the primary numismatic
  scholarship was not opened directly this session.

## Methodology Applied

**Principle 3** applies directly to how this dossier reads the source
material's own citation practice: rather than accepting the *batra*
hadith as automatically supporting a prohibition on *additions*, this
dossier checked what the hadith's own reported wording actually
prohibits (omission of Aal specifically) and found a real gap between
that wording and the broader conclusion drawn from it, treating that gap
as worth flagging explicitly rather than smoothing over because the
individual citations (Sawa'iq al-Muhriqah, al-Shafi'i's statement) check
out. **Principle 4** applies to the coinage argument specifically: it is
evaluated on its own terms as a real methodology with real limits, not
adopted because it arrived bundled with otherwise-accurate citations, and
this dossier found it proves less than the source material uses it to
prove, since the same absence-of-evidence from 7th-century epigraphy cuts
against the Aal-only formula as much as against ashab.
