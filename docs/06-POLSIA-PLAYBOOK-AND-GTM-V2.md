## 1. Aditya's assignment: study Polsia — but verify everything yourself

Zo's ask was specific: go through Ben Broca / Polsia's public posting
history **from the beginning**, find what created the *first* spike of
interest (not what he posts now to maintain rapport with an audience he
already has), note which tools he name-drops, and turn it into a phase-1
playbook to replicate.

That's a reasonable research task. Here's the guide — and the same
warning Zo already gave needs to be said plainly and followed literally:
**do not take any of this at face value. Every claim below came from a
web search of secondary sources (blog posts, one Substack interview,
press coverage, a review site) — not from reading his actual tweets in
order, and not from primary financial documents.** Treat everything here
as a starting hypothesis to verify against his actual X/Twitter timeline
and his own words, not as settled fact to build a strategy on.

### 1.1 What secondary sources say (verify before trusting)

- Polsia launched **December 15, 2025** and reportedly generated revenue
  from day one.
- Reported ARR trajectory (sources disagree on exact figures/timing, which
  is itself the first lesson — see 1.2): ~$500k/mo within ~3 months;
  separately reported as $1M ARR in about a month as a solo founder; a
  month after that, reportedly tripled to $3M ARR; by mid-March 2026 a
  **public, live revenue dashboard** reportedly showed ~$4.1M ARR across
  ~3,900 active companies.
- Founder background: Columbia engineering degree, quant trading at
  Barclays, consumer app ventures, then an operating role at CloudKitchens
  (Travis Kalanick's company) on a unit that scaled to ~$100M revenue.
  Separately, coverage says he moved to San Francisco, "slept on a couch
  for two years," and taught himself to code using AI.
- He spent **2024** — a full year — building various AI tools (coding
  tools, marketing tools) before Polsia existed as a product. He's on
  record saying Opus 4.5's December 2025 release was his "game over"
  moment — i.e., the launch timing lines up with a model-capability jump,
  not only a distribution insight.
- He explicitly names "build in public with real numbers" as his
  philosophy, posts build-in-public updates on X, and is a vocal advocate
  for the "tiny team" thesis (solo founder + AI agents can outperform a
  headcount-heavy startup) — that thesis is arguably his core content
  pillar, not a side note.
- He relocated to SF specifically to be near the AI engineering community.

### 1.2 The first thing this research already proves: don't take it at face value

Even at this shallow, secondary-source level, two things don't fully add
up, and that's the point:

- **Sources disagree on the actual ARR numbers and timing** (three
  different "$1M ARR in X time" framings across different write-ups).
  Growth milestones get rounded, re-dated, and reframed every time they're
  retold. Any specific number Aditya finds needs a primary source (his own
  post, a screenshot with a date on it) before it goes in the playbook as
  a fact.
- **"Slept on a couch for two years teaching himself to code" and "quant
  trader at Barclays → scaled a CloudKitchens unit to $100M" are the same
  person's story, and they pull in different directions.** One is a
  humble-bootstrap narrative; the other is a highly-credentialed operator
  with a serious network (including a direct line to Kalanick's orbit) and
  capital-markets pedigree. Both can be true at once, but a growth
  playbook built only on the couch-and-hustle version will miss that a lot
  of his distribution advantage (who reshares him, who takes his calls,
  who invests $30M in three weeks) came from the network and credibility
  he already had *before* Polsia's first post. That part of the story
  isn't replicable by posting more.

**The instruction to Aditya, explicitly: don't stop at what a blog post
says he did. Read the actual timeline in his own words, and independently
form a view on how much of the outcome is (a) posting tactics, (b) timing
against a real model-capability jump, (c) pre-existing network/pedigree,
and (d) survivorship bias — thousands of people tried some version of
"solo founder + AI agents, build in public" in the same window and didn't
get $30M. Only (a) is actually a transferable "playbook." Say explicitly
in your write-up which parts you think are (a) vs (b)/(c)/(d) — don't let
the write-up imply it's all (a).**

### 1.3 The actual research task (do this, not just this doc)

1. Open his X/Twitter profile and read **from his first post about Polsia
   forward, in order** — not the algorithm's version of his feed, the
   actual chronological timeline. Note the post that got the first
   noticeably-larger-than-his-baseline engagement spike. What was it —
   a number, a demo, a claim, a reply to someone bigger, a screenshot?
2. Separate **"what got the first spike"** from **"what he posts now."**
   Once an account has an audience, almost anything gets engagement — that
   later pattern tells you nothing about how the first 100-1,000 followers
   arrived. Zo's instinct here is exactly right: only the early signal is
   useful.
3. Note every tool/model he explicitly credits (Opus 4.5 is one; there are
   likely others) — not as a growth tactic in itself, but because it tells
   you what capability threshold had to exist for his specific approach to
   work, which tells you whether "posting like him" alone is sufficient
   without that same capability available.
4. Note the **ratio and sequence** of story types: personal narrative vs.
   raw metrics vs. product demo vs. philosophy/thesis posts ("tiny team").
   Reforge's framing in §5 below cares about this sequence, not the exact
   words.
5. Write up the playbook as: **(a) the specific early mechanic**, **(b) what
   pre-existing asset made it work for him that Relay doesn't have**, and
   **(c) the transferable version scaled to a 2-person team with zero
   audience** — not a copy of his cadence. Bring this to the next meet
   before either of you posts anything "in his style."

## 3. The roast (hard)

1. **"Replicate the Polsia playbook" has a survivorship-bias problem
   before it has a tactics problem.** Even with a perfect chronological
   read of his timeline, Aditya would be studying the one visible winner
   from a cohort of an unknown, probably large, number of "solo founder +
   AI agents, build in public" attempts in the same 6-month window that
   went nowhere. A playbook built from n=1 survivors, with no visibility
   into what the failures did differently (probably: the same things), is
   not a playbook — it's a story. Useful for hypothesis generation, not
   for confidence.
2. **His unfair advantages are exactly the parts that don't transfer.**
   Columbia engineering, Barclays quant background, a CloudKitchens
   operating role next to Travis Kalanick that scaled to $100M, and a
   relocation to the specific SF network that reshares and funds people
   like him — none of that is "post like this." A 2-person team with none
   of that pedigree copying his cadence is optimizing the 10% of the
   outcome that was actually posting technique.
3. **Two unrelated distribution bets, in a 2-person team, before either has
   signal, is the exact scope risk `01-ROAST.md` §6 already called out —
   now doubled.** Relay's own GTM (per the existing docs) hadn't run a
   single real cycle yet, and "iamacockroach" is a second, unrelated
   content channel with its own sourcing, its own audience, and its own
   values question, competing for the same two people's time. Pick one to
   run first. Don't run both as "phase 1."
4. **"Outcome is userbase, without agenda" doesn't obviously connect to
   Relay's actual customer.** A GenZ meme-adjacent audience built by
   repurposing someone else's viral content is not the same population as
   "solo founders / small B2B teams who publish content and hate how
   robotic it sounds" — the one ICP the PRD deliberately narrowed to.
   Follower growth on an unrelated channel is a vanity metric relative to
   Relay's actual funnel unless there's a specific, stated hypothesis for
   how that audience becomes Relay customers — and none was stated.
   "We'll use it to dogfood the product later" is a real reason to build a
   *content-repurposing test case*; it is not a reason to call it a GTM
   channel for Relay.
5. **Repurposing another account's content is a legal/brand risk that
   didn't get named.** "Repurpose all [existing accounts'] content" under
   a new persona, without a stated license/attribution/permission
   approach, is a real exposure (platform ToS, copyright, and reputational
   risk if it reads as ripping off a specific creator) — independent of
   whether the content itself is something both founders are comfortable
   putting their name behind, which per Aditya's own comment, they aren't
   fully aligned on yet either.
6. **The "AI subscriptions he uses" signal is weak evidence for the actual
   question.** Knowing his tool stack answers "what's technically
   possible now," not "why did people start paying attention to him." It's
   useful context, not a growth mechanic — worth 10 minutes, not the
   centerpiece of the research.
7. **The concierge instinct ("reach out directly, no product yet, service
   by hand, build as needed") is good — and it's currently just a comment
   in a chat, not a plan with a target list, a message, or a defined
   "quick win."** Good instinct, zero specification. That's the actual gap
   worth closing first, ahead of both the Polsia study and the second
   content channel.

## 5. GTM v2 — the Reforge-style starting point

A Reforge-trained operator doesn't start with "which channel" — they start
by forcing explicit hypotheses across Brian Balfour's **Four Fits**
(Market–Product, Product–Channel, Channel–Model, Model–Market), because a
channel chosen before the other three are even hypothesized is how teams
burn a quarter on the wrong motion. Here's the worksheet, filled with what
this thread actually supports — not fully validated, but explicit instead
of implicit.

### 5.1 Four Fits — current best hypothesis (to be tested, not assumed)

| Fit | Hypothesis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Market–Product** | Solo founders / small B2B teams who already publish and are unhappy with how robotic their AI-assisted content sounds (PRD §2) will value a directable Avatar-based engine over a generic AI writer. | Medium — validated qualitatively at 2 companies (Zo's, Yescity), zero external validation yet. |
| **Product–Channel** | This product needs a **relationship-driven, high-touch** channel initially (someone has to hear "tell me your actual voice/insight," which is a trust-first ask), not a broad paid or viral channel — those fit low-touch, instant-value products, which Relay isn't yet. | High confidence in the *shape*, per Reforge's own product–channel fit logic. |
| **Channel–Model** | A high-touch/concierge channel implies a founder-led, manual-at-first delivery model (see Phase 0) before any self-serve pricing model makes sense — self-serve GTM and concierge GTM are different models, and the existing `04-GTM-DISTRIBUTION.md` wedge tool assumed self-serve too early. | Medium — this is the actual correction this doc is making. |
| **Model–Market** | Once concierge delivery proves willingness-to-pay, a $29-79/mo self-serve tier (per `04-GTM-DISTRIBUTION.md` §6) is a reasonable guess for this market's buying behavior — but that guess is unverified until Phase 0 produces real pricing conversations. | Low — explicitly a placeholder until Phase 0. |

The correction this table makes concrete: **the previous GTM doc's free
self-serve wedge tool assumed Product–Channel and Channel–Model fit that
hasn't been earned yet.** It's not wrong forever — it's Phase 1, not
Phase 0.

### 5.2 Phase 0 (this week, or next) — concierge, using the two accounts you already have

No tool, no signup, no public post. Zo and Aditya personally run the Relay
workflow **by hand** for real content from Zo's own company and from
Yescity:

1. Pick 3-5 real pieces of content either company needs this month (a
   blog post, a founder update, a LinkedIn post).
2. Run each through the actual engine (`packages/engine`/the review UI, or
   even just the underlying prompts by hand if faster) — direct it, review
   it, capture insights into the Avatar, get to an approved multi-format
   output.
3. Deliver the finished formats to whoever owns that content at each
   company, exactly the way a concierge product would — no explanation of
   "there's a tool behind this" required.
4. Ask directly afterward: was this faster/better than what you'd have
   done otherwise? Would you pay for this if it existed as a product?
5. Extend to 5-10 people **outside** the two companies, sourced from Zo's
   and Aditya's own networks, same manual process, same two questions.
   This is Zo's "direct outreach, no product, service them, build as
   needed" instinct, executed instead of implied.

**Exit criteria**: at least 8-10 of the ~15 people say it was clearly
better/faster, and at least 3 say "I'd pay for this" unprompted — same
bar `03-PRD.md` §8 already set, just reached manually before any wedge
tool exists, per Reforge's Product–Channel logic above.

### 5.3 Phase 1 — only after Phase 0 signal: turn the proven manual motion into a channel

- Now build the self-serve **Voice Sample** wedge from
  `04-GTM-DISTRIBUTION.md` §2 — it's a good design, it was just sequenced
  too early. Its job is to scale a *proven* manual motion, not to
  discover whether the motion works.
- This is where the Polsia research (§1, reframed) actually gets used:
  take the specific, falsifiable mechanics identified there — not his
  cadence, the underlying mechanic (e.g., "lead with a real number/demo
  before a thesis post," if that's what the chronological read shows) —
  and test them in Relay's own build-in-public thread, as an explicit,
  named experiment, not a copy.
- Yescity and Zo's own company become the first two case studies/
  testimonials referenced in outreach — real proof, not a hypothetical.

### 5.4 The GenZ channel, if it happens — explicitly separate

If "iamacockroach" proceeds, it gets its own one-page plan with its own
success metric (e.g., "1,000 real followers in 3 weeks," not "engagement
without agenda"), a resolved answer on content sourcing (not "whatever
works"), and an explicit non-goal: it is not counted toward Relay's Phase
0/1 numbers unless a specific test later shows audience overlap. Keep it
out of the core motion's success criteria.

### 5.5 Concrete next deliverable (this replaces the open "draft a basic MVP plan" ask)

Aditya's committed deliverable, made specific:

1. A named list of 10-15 real people (not personas) across Zo's and
   Aditya's networks, plus the 2 companies, for Phase 0 concierge outreach.
2. A one-paragraph "quick win" offer per person/company — what gets
   delivered by hand, in what format, by when.
3. The two exit-criteria questions from §5.2, asked the same way every
   time so the results are comparable.
4. The Polsia write-up from §1.3(5) — mechanic, unfair advantage, and
   transferable version — brought to the next meet, separately from the
   above, since it feeds Phase 1 rather than Phase 0.
