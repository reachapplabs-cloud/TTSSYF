# Research Methodology & Guardrail Principles

**Read this before anything else in this repo.** These are the standing
rules this whole compilation — past chapters and any future research —
is bound by. They came directly from the project owner as a correction to
how source material should be weighed, and they apply retroactively as a
*lens* on everything already written here (the verbatim quotes in
`chapters/` are not rewritten to match them — see "How this interacts
with existing chapters" below) and prospectively to everything written
after this point. Nothing in this project should ever skip these checks.

## Principle 1 — Rijal grading is not self-authenticating

Classical *rijal* (narrator-biography) science is a human discipline, not
revelation. This project does **not** treat "recorded in Bukhari" or
"recorded in Muslim" — even both together, as *al-Sahihain* — as
equivalent to, or near, revelation. A hadith being graded *sahih* by
chain-of-narration criteria establishes only that the classical science
judged the chain sound by its own rules; it does not by itself establish
that the Prophet ﷺ actually said it.

The same skepticism runs in both directions: where a narrator is labeled
untrustworthy — called a liar (*kadhdhab*), weak (*da'if*), or accused of
losing his memory (*ikhtalata* / *sayyi' al-hifz*) — that grading is
**also** not accepted automatically. Such rulings can themselves be
politically motivated (made by scholars operating under, or sympathetic
to, a particular dynasty or faction), so a narrator-criticism is a claim
to be examined, not a fact to be cited. In short: neither "he's
authenticated" nor "he's discredited" is taken at face value — both the
narration and any verdict on its narrator get the same scrutiny.

## Principle 2 — No transmitter is above scrutiny, fame included

Applied first-principles, without exception for how celebrated a narrator
is. This project does not extend a pass to a narrator on the strength of
his reputation; every report is still tested against the Qur'an, internal
consistency, and known controverted incidents involving that narrator —
Abu Hurairah included, despite being the most prolific and widely relied
upon narrator in the Sunni corpus.

Two concrete cases were raised as the standard this principle should meet
(both flagged here as open items needing precise citation in the
"detailed research" phase mentioned below — not yet independently
verified by this project, stated as claims for now):

- **Creation in seven days.** A hadith holds that the world was created
  in seven days — which conflicts with the Qur'an's own account of
  creation in six days (see the existing verbatim discussion of this
  exact hadith already in this repo at
  [`chapters/02-sunnah-and-hadith-authenticity.md`, §2.1](chapters/02-sunnah-and-hadith-authenticity.md)
  ("If a hadith's own chain is disputed by senior Sunni scholars..."),
  where Bukhari is quoted as attributing the wording to Ka'b al-Ahbar
  rather than the Prophet ﷺ, even though Muslim carried it). The isnad is
  identified here as running through Abu Hurairah — this project treats
  that as reason to weigh the report against the Qur'an rather than defer
  to its inclusion in Muslim.
- **"Salah is invalidated by a dog, donkey, or woman passing in front."**
  Narrated by Abu Hurairah, and reported to have been directly rebuked by
  Aisha (RA) herself, who is said to have objected to being equated with
  a dog or donkey and to have cited her own experience of lying in front
  of the Prophet ﷺ while he prayed as a counter-proof. Added as a new
  question at
  [`chapters/03-salah-and-worship-practices.md`, §3.2](chapters/03-salah-and-worship-practices.md).

This same posture — fame is not a shield — applies to every narrator this
project studies, not to Abu Hurairah alone; he is simply the sharpest
illustration of why the rule exists.

## Principle 3 — Watch for concealment patterns: "fulan," "kalima," "kadha wa kaza," and silent truncation

Where Bukhari (or any collector) replaces a name with *fulan* ("so-and-so"),
replaces a phrase with *kalima* ("a word") or *kadha wa kaza*
("such-and-such"), or otherwise cuts a hadith short without the full
wording, this project treats that as worth calling out explicitly, not
passing over as a stylistic quirk. The suspicion driving this rule: a
redaction like this can be evidence that something politically sensitive
was deliberately obscured, and the fact of the redaction is itself
evidence worth recording, independent of whatever the missing content
turns out to be. This applies across the whole project, to every
collection studied, not to Bukhari alone.

No instance of this pattern has been logged yet in this repo's existing
chapters (none of the source material processed so far happened to
contain one) — it is recorded here as a standing rule so that any future
research that encounters it flags it rather than silently normalizing it.

## How these guardrails apply to this repo

- **Existing chapters are not rewritten.** The verbatim quotes already in
  `chapters/01`–`07` are the project owner's original forwarded material
  and stay exactly as received, per the no-omission/no-rewording rule in
  [`QA-CHECKLIST.md`](QA-CHECKLIST.md). These principles are layered on
  top as editorial annotations next to the relevant verbatim text (see
  the note added to Chapter 2, §2.1), never as edits to the quoted text
  itself.
- **New material added under this methodology is explicitly marked** as
  such, with a "Source: added by [date], not from the original document"
  line — distinct from the "Source: `raw-transcript.md`, message
  timestamped..." lines used for the original docx content — so the
  provenance of every claim in this repo stays checkable at a glance.
- **Anything asserted without a verified citation is marked "NEEDS
  VERIFICATION"** rather than stated as settled — including this
  project's own additions. The same "don't take it at face value" rule
  applies reflexively to material this project generates, not only to
  material it's critiquing.

## Open research items (for the "detailed research" phase)

The project owner indicated a follow-up phase is wanted: quoting,
referencing, and linking sources without bias to reach a better-grounded
consensus on the points above. Tracked here so it isn't lost:

1. Verify the exact isnad and citation for the Abu Hurairah "creation in
   seven days" hadith in Sahih Muslim, and cross-check it against
   Bukhari's *Tarikh al-Kabir* attribution of the wording to Ka'b
   al-Ahbar (already partly documented at Chapter 2, §2.1, via the Ibn
   Taymiyyah/Al-Mu'allimi/Al-Albani debate).
2. Verify the exact citation for Aisha's rebuke of Abu Hurairah on the
   dog/donkey/woman prayer-invalidation hadith (candidates to check:
   Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abu Dawud, Musnad Ahmad).
3. Build a logged inventory — across Bukhari and other collections
   relevant to this research — of instances using *fulan*, *kalima*,
   *kadha wa kaza*, or other truncation, with hadith numbers, as they are
   found.
4. Case-by-case review of specific rijal-criticism entries (e.g. a
   narrator called *matruk*, *kadhib*, or *sayyi' al-hifz*) to assess
   whether the criticism itself traces to a scholar or period with a
   plausible political motive, rather than accepting the grading as
   neutral.

This list should grow as research continues — it is a starting point, not
a fixed scope.
